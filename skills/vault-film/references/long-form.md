# 1 to 8 minutes

The vault's references are mostly 40–65 s Shorts or 2–4 min films. A longer film is not a stretched Short: it is several chapters, each with its own small question, held together by one spine.

## Sizing

| Length | Words (explainer ~155 WPM / chronicler ~135 WPM) | Chapters | Shots |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 min | 150 / 135 | 1–2 | 10–25 |
| 3 min | 460 / 400 | 3–4 | 30–60 |
| 5 min | 770 / 675 | 5–6 | 50–100 |
| 8 min | 1,240 / 1,080 | 7–9 | 80–160 |

The lint sizes the real script and the voice measures it; these are for the brief. A silent film is sized by its music instead.

## Structure

- The hook lands in the first 4 s and the film's real question inside the first 30–45 s.
- Each chapter is 45–90 s (20–90 s in a 1–2 min film), makes one move (one mechanism layer, one stage, one scene) and ends on a rehook: an open question, a reversal or a "but" that the next chapter answers.
- One dry turn for the whole film, in the last third. Chapters before it may breathe (a `*(beat)*`), but only one line lands with the music out.
- Vary the pace between chapters: a fast-cut chapter after a held one, one clear acceleration, one breath. A film at one even speed has failed.
- The ending echoes the opening image, changed by what the viewer now knows.

## Working per chapter

1. Script, lint and voice the whole film first; the picture is timed to the whole voice.
2. Plan all chapters (`film.json`) and get the look proof approved before building any of them.
3. Build one chapter fully (stills, review, render) before starting the next, in story order, so the look settles early. Send the person a contact sheet per finished chapter; do not wait for the whole film.
4. Keep a running `review/notes.md`: what each chapter established (a character's colours, a camera habit) that later chapters must match.
5. Mix and assemble once every chapter exists; a single changed chapter afterwards is re-rendered alone and re-joined.

## Honest effort

Tell the person at intake, before they choose a length:

- A code-drawn chapter is real authoring: plan on a working session per 45–90 s chapter for a schematic explainer and more for acted character animation. A 6–8 min code-drawn film is several long sessions.
- Stills-based chapters (HyperFrames) are much faster to build, but the person has to make one image per 5–12 s (about 40–60 images for 7 min).
- Renders: light Remotion and canvas chapters render faster than real time at 1080p on this machine; heavy drawing, and three.js above all, is many times slower. Time one chapter before promising a date.
- Past 3 minutes, suggest the cheaper mix first: stills or a continuous-canvas explainer for most chapters, code-drawn set pieces for the two or three moments that carry the film.

## Series

A recurring cast or a numbered saga is the cheapest way to grow: the rigs, palette, voices and score motifs carry over. Keep them in the project folder, note in `brief.md` what the next part inherits, and end each part on the unresolved choice the next part opens with.
