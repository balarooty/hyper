# Story and narration script

Read this before writing `script.md`. It applies to every narrated video; `formats.md` has each format's spine and beat map, and `dialogue.md` covers characters who speak.

## 0. Four laws

1. **Scene over poster.** A poster is looked at; a scene is entered. Open with a named human (or a specific everyman), a concrete prop, a lived wound and a choice that costs something. For an explainer, open on a concrete case instead of an abstraction. Never open on passive spectacle, preaching or throat-clearing exposition.
2. **The value turn lives in the hand.** Emotion comes from a physical choice made visible on one object: a fist opening around a card, new shoes left in a dark closet with the tag on, a scribe laying down his stylus. If you can't see the turn in a hand or a prop, the scene isn't working.
3. **Silence and subtext carry the weight.** Never narrate what the picture and foley already prove. Total silence (the dry turn) lands exactly once, on the turning point.
4. **Frame 0 is the hook.** No logo, no "hey guys", no title card in the first 2 seconds. A Short's last frame matches its first, so the loop replays seamlessly.

## 1. Spine first (write it in `brief.md`; it is never spoken)

- **Premise**: trait or force + cause + consequence. ("Trusting one big box leads to confusion until you see the four parts.")
- **Controlling idea**: the value the ending proves, and why. Write the ending or twist first, then work backward.
- **Want vs. misbelief**: what the viewer (or hero) wants, and the false belief blocking it. The video breaks that belief.
- **Four Decisions** (story formats):

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Human | Name, age, a specific job, and the lie they believe ("Daniel, 34, night-shift bricklayer; believes he must fix himself before he can walk into his late father's church") |
| Prop | One object that holds the promise, the failure and the turn |
| Value turn | The starting state → the ending state, shown as a physical action ("closed fist → open palm") |
| Anchor word | One quote, verse or principle that lands after the turn, never as decoration at the start |

For a story with a hero (Shorts of 45 s and up), run the 7 steps compressed, one beat each where possible: weakness/need → concrete desire → opponent (as strong as the hero) → plan → battle → self-revelation → new equilibrium proved by a choice.

★ Before writing the script, offer **3 storylines**, each with its hook archetype and hook line, a 3-line spine and the ending line. The person picks one.

## 2. The hook: lock one archetype before writing the body

| # | Archetype | Best for | Mechanism | Strong (illustration only; never reuse the line) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The two-second hand | faith and emotional Shorts | at 0:00 a hand does something unexplained with a loaded prop, plus one line of wounded subtext | MACRO: a thumb over a two-year-old unplayed voicemail. "My dad left me a voicemail." / *(beat)* / "I never pressed play." |
| 2 | The guilt mirror | habit and behaviour films | a private failure the viewer committed today, inside 5 s | 2:14 a.m. phone glare on tired eyes, alarm set for 6:00, one more reel; no words, a clock ticking |
| 3 | The Sunday-school disruption | lore, myth, history | state what everyone was taught, then the gritty textual reality | "Every Sunday school says the Flood came because people were bad. Nobody mentions the two hundred Watchers on Mount Hermon." |
| 4 | The forbidden reactance | apocrypha, science essays | what councils, history or evolution left out, only when literally true and sourced | "Most Bibles left this book out. The early church quoted it anyway." |
| 5 | The internal crisis | comedy, illustrated parables | straight into a sensory pressure point or an internal rebellion | "Day four of ten thousand steps: your legs have filed a formal complaint." |
| E | The counter-intuitive fact | explainers | the absurd fact, stated plainly, with a head fake waiting | "Ferrari sold thirteen thousand cars and made six billion euros." |

Weak, never: "Today let's talk about…", "In this video…", "Have you ever wondered…", "In a world where…", "Did you know…". The lint flags them.

**The hook loop** (every video, every section):

1. **Stakes**: a viewpoint with something to lose, and a clock if possible.
2. **Big question**: enough concrete context that the viewer forms a guess.
3. **Head fake**: break that guess, surprising now and obvious in hindsight.
4. **Rehook**: close the loop and open the next in the same breath ("Which would've been fine, except…"). In long-form, every section (45–90 s) ends on a rehook, not a summary.

## 3. Narration formula (explainers; story formats follow `formats.md`)

1. **Hook** (sentence 1, no setup): the absurd or counter-intuitive fact.
2. **Setting** in 1–2 lines: when, where, who, one concrete detail.
3. **Escalation**: 2–4 beats, each bigger. Someone doubts or mocks. One exaggeration joke.
4. **Pivot**: "But when…" / "You see…" introduces the real mechanism, often through an everyday analogy.
5. **Twist or payoff**: short, ironic, the last words of the video; it carries the `*(dry)*` cue.

## 5. Line rules

- Plain spoken English, contractions, sentences of about 8–15 words. Numbers written as words; acronyms as spoken ("L L M").
- **Cadence, long-long-short**: two medium-to-long sentences that build sensory detail and movement, then a 3–6-word hammer. ("Before the Watchers came, men fought with fists and stones. After the iron, brother cut down brother with a sword." / *(beat)* / "And the earth cried out.") Every scene has a hammer, and never five same-length sentences in a row (the lint checks).
- **Verbs and nouns over adjectives**: no intensifiers ("very sad", "extremely terrifying"). Let a concrete noun and a physical verb carry it: not "he was devastated", but "the old king wrapped his head in coarse wool, climbed the stairs above the gate and wept until his soldiers were ashamed to enter the city".
- Never describe what the viewer already sees; say what the picture can't. Concrete nouns (props, places, bodies) beat abstractions ("trust", "efficiency").
- After any heavy fact, the next line is a human reaction or consequence, not another fact (exposition buffer).
- **The one sentence that could reach them**: at the turn, a character says only the single sentence that speaker could say in that exact second. No small talk, no lecture. (✗ "Daniel, I forgive you for missing church before your father passed away. Come inside now." ✓ "I know you were late, Daniel." / *(beat)* / "I kept the pew.")
- **Anchor after the turn**: the verse, quote or moral never opens the film; it lands after the human action has earned it. Mark it `*(anchor)*` if spoken. Name the translation of any scripture (public domain: KJV, WEB; others short, with their attribution).
- **Ethical CTA**: an invitation that echoes the film's motif ("Open your hand. Type AMEN if you're still learning to trust."), never guilt or threat ("skip this and you don't love God"). Long-form: one line at the end; Shorts: on-screen text at the loop point, not spoken.
- No "In this video…", "Let's dive in", "Hey guys", "Without further ado". Start on the move.
- No stage directions in the narration except delivery cues (§6).

## 6. Delivery cues

Every scene carries at least one cue, and the script has exactly one `dry`. `voice.py` performs them; the lint checks them; they are never spoken.

| Cue | Meaning | Voice | Picture and sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| `*(beat)*` | hold, let the last noun echo | 0.9–1.2 s silence (persona) instead of the gap | the picture holds or moves; no busy cut |
| `*(beat — the fist closes around the card)*` | the pause and the action are one moment | same | the action lands on this beat (shotlist cue table) |
| `*(inhale)*` | a breath before a painful line | 0.45 s pause | optional breath foley |
| `*(drop register)*`, `*(almost a whisper)*`, `*(whisper)*` | the listener has to lean in | that line −4.4 dB, slower | keep the picture intimate |
| `*(dry — cut music)*` | the turning point, unguarded | — | music out ≥ 1.5 s, reverb off, foley stays |
| `*(anchor)*` | the verse/moral line | — | the anchor holds still on screen |

Placement: a cue on its own line acts on the gap after the previous line (beat) or on the next line (the others); at the start or end of a line it acts on that line. A cue never sits mid-line (split the line: each line is one voice take). Lines voiced by another voice are tagged `sNN_lNN [NAME]: …`.

```
## s02 The turn
s02_l01: He tried to pray. He couldn't get past the name.
*(beat — the fist closes around the card)*
s02_l02 [VOICE]: *(dry — cut music)* I know you were late, Daniel.
*(beat)*
s02_l03: *(drop register)* Nobody told me grace keeps the seat.
```

## 7. Beats (write them in `shotlist.md` before the shots)

List 6–15 beats. Each beat is a **change** (in value, situation or knowledge) with `description`, `emotion`, `seconds`. Adjacent beats must differ in emotion, place or action.

End every section on one of: a revelation, a decision, a symbolic image, a mid-action cut, an emotional shift. A quiet pause comes only after the big resolution. Never a sleepy fade-out.

## 8. Craft check (what the lint can't see), before showing a script

1. Human first: name, age, a specific job, the lie they believe, a prop in the hand. Explainers: one concrete case or viewpoint, not "people".
2. The value turn is visible in a hand or an object.
3. One prop carries the promise, the failure and the turn.
4. The anchor is earned: it lands after the turn.
5. The line at the turn is the one sentence only that speaker could say in that second.
6. The CTA is an invitation, not guilt.
7. Long-form: every word is written (about 950 for 7 minutes), not an outline.
8. Original: a new human, prop, lines and shots; only the grammar of any reference.
9. Every fact is in `sources.md`; disputed or legendary material is said plainly.

## 9. Deliver the script as

1. The script in a copyable block, with word count and estimated length.
2. A one-paragraph visual plan (the shots, the light arc, the sound bed).
3. Before showing it, run the lint and fix every warning:

```bash
python3 -I ~/.claude/skills/video-studio/scripts/lint_script.py script.md --seconds <target> [--persona <name>]
```
