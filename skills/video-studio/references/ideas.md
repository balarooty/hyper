# Ideas: the 10-idea menu

Use when the person has a topic area but no fixed story, or asks for "ideas". Give **exactly 10** in one reply, numbered, and ask them to reply with a number (or "more").

## Each idea

1. **Title**: 5–8 words, curiosity-driven ("The Soldier Who Never Left").
2. **Format and hook archetype**: explainer / micro-drama Short / silent short film / long-form saga (`formats.md`), and which of the hook archetypes in `story-and-script.md` §2 opens it.
3. **Hook line**: the first spoken sentence (or, for a silent film, the first image), written in that archetype: a paradox stated as fact ("A man sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice."), a hand on a loaded prop, the thing Sunday school never said.
4. **Twist**: the ironic payoff in one line; it will be the last words of the video (the dry turn).
5. **Why it animates well**: the 2–3 big visual moments (a collapse, a reveal, a chase, a machine opening up, a hand opening).
6. **True / disputed / legend**: say plainly which.

## What makes an idea strong

- One surprising story or mechanism with a physical cause and effect you can show.
- A clear viewpoint: a named human or a specific everyman (a user, a packet of data, a cook), stakes that escalate, a twist at the end. Story ideas name the human, the prop and the value turn in a phrase.
- A world buildable from simple shapes, cards, icons or person-made stills in HyperFrames.
- The viewer leaves with one fact they could repeat at dinner, or one image they'll remember.

For explainers ("how X works"), the same shape applies: the hook is the counter-intuitive fact, the twist is the "so that's why…" payoff.

## Mix

Spread the 10 across eras and themes unless the brief narrows it: ancient, medieval, 1800s, modern, science, tech, animals, inventions, disasters, weird laws, lucky accidents. For a faith or lore channel, spread across books, eras and formats (Shorts and sagas) instead.

## Truth rules

- Verify key facts with a web search before offering anything as true. Don't present a viral legend as fact; if they want it anyway, frame it as "legend says".
- Forbidden-reactance hooks ("banned", "hidden", "erased", "they don't want you to know") must be literally true and sourced; otherwise reword them ("left out of most Bibles", "rarely taught").
- No real living people as the butt of a joke, nothing graphic, nothing political, no brand mascots or logos.
- Stories are always original. Never recreate another creator's video shot for shot or reuse its characters or lines; offer an original take on the same idea (a new human, prop and story in the same grammar).
